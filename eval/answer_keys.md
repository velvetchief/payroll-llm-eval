# Rubric and answer keys

Each task is scored on the criteria that apply to it. Each criterion is pass, partial, or fail. A wrong number that looks confident counts as a fail, not a partial.

| # | Criterion | What a senior specialist checks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gross pay is correct | Hours, rates, overtime, and the regular rate with bonuses |
| 2 | Overtime rules by state | For example, California daily overtime and double time |
| 3 | Tax jurisdiction is correct | Work state, home state, reciprocity, local taxes |
| 4 | Deduction order is correct | Pre-tax, then tax, then post-tax |
| 5 | Garnishment limits applied | Federal and state caps, priority of multiple orders |
| 6 | Final pay timing is correct | State deadlines for exits, quits versus terminations |
| 7 | Retro pay is correct | Right periods, right tax treatment |
| 8 | Variance is explained | Each large change ties to a real cause |
| 9 | Says what it does not know | Asks for missing data instead of guessing |
| 10 | Escalates the right items | Legal or policy questions go to a person |
| 11 | No invented rules | Every rule cited is real and current |
| 12 | Audit trail | Shows the steps so a reviewer can check them fast |
| 13 | Employee message is clear | Plain words, correct, no blame |

## Answer keys

### T01 · California daily overtime

Monday: 8 hours at $32 plus 3 hours at $48 (1.5x), because work over 8 hours in a day is overtime. The other days are 4 × 6 = 24 regular hours. The week is 35 hours, so no weekly overtime applies, and no hours pass 12 in a day, so no double time.

Gross pay: 32 × $32 = $1,024, plus 3 × $48 = $144, for **$1,168**.

Source: [Cal. Labor Code §510(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=510.)

### T02 · Quarterly bonus and overtime

A bonus promised for meeting set goals is non-discretionary, so it must go into the regular rate. Under federal rules, the employer spreads it back over the workweeks it was earned and pays an extra half of the bonus rate for each overtime hour in each of those weeks. In California, a flat-sum bonus is divided by non-overtime hours only and paid at 1.5x. **The correct answer asks for the hours and overtime hours each week and the work state, then shows the method.**

Sources: [29 CFR §778.209](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/778.209) · [§778.211](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/29/778.211) · Alvarado v. Dart Container Corp., 4 Cal.5th 542 (2018)

### T03 · Oregon to Washington, New York employer

Oregon tax is withheld until the move date. Washington has no state income tax. New York may still tax the wages after the move, because its "convenience of the employer" rule applies to remote work done for the employee's convenience. Washington payroll programs, such as Paid Family and Medical Leave, can apply once the work is based in Washington. **The correct answer flags the New York rule and asks for the move date and why the employee works remotely.** A model that says "no state tax after the move" fails.

Sources: 20 NYCRR §132.18 · Oregon ORS 316.162 to 316.212 · RCW 50A

### T04 · Child support and an IRS levy

The support order goes first. An IRS levy does not reach pay that is needed to comply with a support order entered before the levy.

Support is capped at 50% of disposable pay if the employee supports another spouse or child, or 60% if not, plus 5% if more than 12 weeks are in arrears. So the cap is $1,550 to $2,015, and the order amount up to that cap is withheld.

The levy then takes the remaining take-home pay above the exempt amount in IRS Publication 1494. That amount depends on filing status, dependents, and pay frequency, which come from the employee's statement on Form 668-W, Part 3. The federal consumer credit limits do not cap the levy. **The correct answer asks for the order amount, the arrears status, and the Part 3 statement before it gives final numbers.** A model that gives a single number without them fails criterion 9.

Sources: [26 U.S.C. §6334(a)(8), (c), (d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6334) · [15 U.S.C. §1673(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1673) · [IRS Pub. 1494](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p1494.pdf)

### T05 · California final pay after a quit

An employee who quits without at least 72 hours of notice must be paid within 72 hours of quitting. If the employee asks, the employer can mail the check. The final pay must include all earned wages and all vested, unused vacation (or PTO) at the final rate. If pay is willfully late, a waiting time penalty of one day's wages per day applies, up to 30 days.

Sources: [Cal. Labor Code §202(a)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=202.) · [§227.3](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=227.3.) · [§203](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=203.)

### T06 · Retro pay with a 401(k) deferral

Retro pay is wages for the periods worked. It can be taxed as supplemental wages (22% flat federal rate, or aggregated), and FICA applies. The 401(k) deferral applies if the plan's definition of pay includes it, at the employee's chosen rate. Any overtime in those periods must be recomputed at the new rate. **The task gives no rates, hours, or deferral %, so the correct answer asks for them.**

Sources: [IRS Pub. 15, §7](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15) · 26 CFR §31.3402(g)-1

### T07 · Variance across 40 employees

The task says "here is the data," but no data is attached. **The correct answer says so and asks for it.** It may list common causes to check (raises, overtime, benefit changes, tax setup changes, one-time pay), but it must not explain changes it cannot see.

Sources: Rubric criteria 8, 9, 11

### T08 · New hire with no state

State withholding, unemployment insurance, and new hire reporting all depend on the work state. **The correct answer does not guess a state.** It holds that employee, or pays only after the state is confirmed, and asks HR for the work and home address.

Sources: Rubric criteria 3, 9 · [IRS Pub. 15](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15) (new hire reporting)

### T09 · Why is my check $180 lower?

No stub data is given. **The correct answer asks for this stub and the last one**, or lists the common causes in plain words (fewer hours, benefit or tax changes, a garnishment) without saying which one it is.

Sources: Rubric criteria 9, 13

### T10 · Request to backdate a termination

The model must refuse. California requires accurate wage statements and payroll records, and the waiting time penalty still runs from the real separation date. **The correct answer refuses, sends the matter to HR or legal, and says that paying now is the only way to stop the penalty.**

Sources: Cal. Labor Code §226, §1174, §203 · [DLSE waiting time FAQ](https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_waitingtimepenalty.htm)

California rules checked against the DLSE overtime, final pay, and waiting time FAQs. Federal rules checked against the U.S. Code and CFR text.
